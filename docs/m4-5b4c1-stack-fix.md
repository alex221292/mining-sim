# M4.5b.4c.1 — stack support and reactivation

Group reactivation previously rejected any existing occupancy in a transferred cell. Occupancy is a conservative area index: a sleeping body resting on that cell can already penetrate it slightly under the existing contact solver. A reproduced small stack had 0.0117869 cells of penetration. Group preparation now performs bounded actual occupied-cell SAT checks against existing bodies. A sleeping pre-existing contact up to the already established held-penetration limit (0.1 cells) remains a contact for the subsequent physics step; it is not erased or moved. Moving overlaps and deeper penetrations are rejected with body IDs and penetration information. The prepared occupancy retains both IDs. No SAT, solver coefficient or sleep rule was changed. This does not permit arbitrary interpenetration or guarantee recovery from unrelated SWEEP failures.

The old assembly check required every possible upper-contact point to lie inside the lower support interval, even where the actual combined load and moment are balanced. Vertical support now propagates known integer masses and moments from upper nodes to support contacts in deterministic order, while unknown ordinary-STONE loads remain conservatively bounded. A separate prospective analysis includes candidate cells without changing World. Grounded contacts are resolved before load distribution; unsupported supports are not used. Cyclic, indeterminate or unsupported configurations remain conservative refusals. The analysis is bounded and may reject feasible configurations because it chooses deterministic extreme reactions rather than solving every possible force distribution. On rejection, the reason includes candidate and settled IDs, node mass, center, support interval, total load, moment and resultant. F3 displays the beginning of that reason.

The earlier test that called a single offset upper cell destabilizing was corrected: its actual resultant lay inside the lower support. The replacement uses a three-cell upper rock whose resultant reaches the edge of the support interval and is genuinely rejected. Tests also cover an offset three-rock stack that settles and remains static, support loss with a sleeping rigid above it and subsequent falling, an independent rigid, a real external overlap, veto/stale plans, deterministic replay and the 134/134/526 lifecycle. GUI manual acceptance remains pending.

## Measurement

OpenJDK 25.0.5+8-LTS, Windows; `java -cp build/classes/java/main miningsim.benchmark.ProspectiveSupportBenchmark 10`, one JVM, two warmups, 20×20 for ten runs. Mean/p95 ms: first preparation 0.197/0.285, second 0.256/0.394, third 0.280/0.393 (10 each), cascade tick 0.313/0.495 (10), unchanged three-rock tick 0.010/0.013 (1,200). One 128×64 dense run with 20 settled rocks: unchanged tick 0.019/0.024 (120), STONE edit tick 0.058/0.093 (10), SAND ticks 0.273/0.416 (180). The latter includes motion after STONE edits. These wall-clock timings are not FPS or guarantees.
