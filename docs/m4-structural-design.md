# M4.0 — Structural Physics & Construction: design

Historical M4.0 snapshot: M4.1 is now implemented; see `m4-1-bedrock.md` for current behavior.

**Status:** design only. Confirmed decisions below are product constraints; all algorithms, staged interfaces, fixture dimensions and migration procedures labeled *proposed* require validation in later milestones. No M4 code, new material ID, file version or controls exist yet. M4.1–M4.10 are independently reviewable slices, not a promise of physical accuracy or a commitment to a particular external physics library.

## 1. Existing system and integration boundary

- `miningsim.world.World` owns bounded, eagerly allocated 64×64 storage chunks and exposes checked world-coordinate `get`, `set`, `clear`; IDs 0–6 are defined in `Material`. `STONE` and `WOOD` are static **cells** today; neither bedrock nor body entities exist. A `World.RowSegment` is a live read view, not a structural model. Current tests, including pre-M3.2 hash `0x616e60ae6b94de46L`, remain valid until a separately approved milestone intentionally changes behavior.
- `miningsim.simulation.Simulation.step()` is headless, bottom-up and seed-driven; `FULL_SCAN` is default. Optional 32×32 activity tiles wake from cell changes; `ACTIVE_REGIONS` can differ in scheduling and random consumption. This mechanism is not a support solver, storage chunk scheduler or structural event queue.
- `Main` and `SimulationControl` run input, stepping and Java2D painting on the Swing EDT with an accumulator targeting 60 TPS, capped catch-up, pause and single-step. `Renderer` uses `Graphics2D`; `Camera2D` and `Brush` map screen to world. The renderer observes state and cannot determine physics or advance time.
- `miningsim.persistence.WorldIO` reads/writes MSIM v1 (bounded cell IDs and CRC32 only); it does not save random state, tick, damage, bodies, contacts or construction. Current `World.set` does not emit structural events; future excavation/placement must introduce an explicit, testable mutation boundary before structural analysis is trusted. Existing mouse painting/erase/clear can currently replace any cell; bedrock invariants therefore require a future, intentional change across **all** mutation entry points, not just a new brush rule.

## 2. Confirmed constraints and proposed model

**Confirmed:** one cell represents 0.1 m. `BEDROCK` exists only on the bottom boundary and is indestructible. `STONE` forms finite-strength, potentially self-supporting structures. Intact static stone stays in `World`; falling stone fragments and wooden beams are separate rigid entities, not falling material-cell collections. Orthogonally adjacent stone cells have structural bonds; connectivity is necessary for a load path but does **not** establish mechanical stability. Geometry, thickness, span, loads, intact bonds and supports matter. Moderate overload accumulates persistent damage and visible cracks; extreme overload may fail immediately; adding support does not heal damage. Detached stone becomes a whole rigid fragment, which may rotate and break on impact. Structural propagation is event-driven and camera-independent. A cell-resolution local model is the initial reference; adaptive elements are a later measured optimization, not a promise of exact FEM.

**Proposed reference approximation:** represent each undirected orthogonal stone–stone bond once using a canonical ordered pair of world coordinates, with explicit intact/damaged/broken state and bounded monotone damage. Treat bedrock as an immutable external support boundary, not a source of infinite material. Evaluate gravity/self-weight and applied contact forces/torques, distributing loads through eligible supported bonds and contacts using a specified bounded local iterative approximation; include bending/span, section thickness and accumulated damage, rather than a connectivity-only or pure vertical-column check. Exact equations, convergence rules, crack rendering threshold, quantization and calibration are open and must be specified/tested in M4.2–M4.3 before being represented as physics. A connectivity pass may identify candidate components and support-path topology; it is **not** a stability verdict. It cannot substitute for load transfer, moment, failure and damage calculations.

**Proposed adaptive optimization:** aggregate only compatible intact cells into structural elements, with conservative mass, geometry and bond/interface representation; refine near cracks, excavation, support/contact changes and high gradients. Compare against the cell reference on identical initial conditions, inputs and tick boundaries, including mass, load reactions, damage/failure time and topology with predeclared tolerances. Coarsening can smear stress concentration, distort bending stiffness or crack path, and alter failure time; never claim numerical equivalence merely because a component remains connected. Refine or reject an approximation when error exceeds declared tolerances. Parameter values and error bounds are unresolved, not silently chosen here.

## 3. Responsibilities and ownership (proposed)

| Component | Owns / authoritative state | Boundary and responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| World | Bounded cell materials and checked cell mutations | Static stone/bedrock and existing materials; no rigid bodies or rendering state. World coordinates are stable across storage chunks. |
| Mutation coordinator / simulation orchestrator | Tick index, ordered accepted commands and deterministic structural work queue | Sole authority for applying structural edits; emits explicit deltas for cell changes, fragment conversion, placement, contacts and failures. No wall-clock budgeting. |
| Structural state and reference analysis | Canonical bond identities, persistent damage, anchored support analysis and solver progress | Consumes committed deltas and contact loads, produces staged damage/failure and fragments; no camera dependence. Separate from 32×32 activity and renderer dirtiness. |
| Construction and placement | Fabricated beam provenance/length, preview, snap intent and temporary assembly records | Validates geometry and resources via explicit commands; preview is non-authoritative; no automatic beam–beam joint. No assumed inventory implementation. |
| Attachments and joints | Stable IDs, endpoints, anchors, strength, accumulated damage | Rock attachment is a replaceable policy; explicit player-installed `BEAM_JOINT` is distinct from contact. Publishes constraints and reaction loads. |
| Rigid-body dynamics | Bodies, shapes, mass/inertia, poses, velocities, contacts and impulses | Fragments and beams; fixed-step deterministic contact ordering. Does not own static `World` cells. |
| Persistence | Versioned validated authoritative snapshots | Serializes at defined tick boundary; no renderer cache or derived graph required; v1 compatibility is explicit. |
| Rendering / input | Presentation, camera and input-to-command translation | Renders cells, bodies and crack state; may cull visuals, never structural events. UI does not mutate model concurrently. |

Names above are conceptual responsibilities, **not** required classes or current APIs. Any future bodies use stable IDs assigned deterministically, not object identity or unordered-map iteration; arrays/compact structures are preferred where measured. Contact, attachment and structural data must identify the same owners rather than independently duplicating mass or state.

## 4. Proposed fixed tick and bounded work

At each explicit 1/60 s logical tick, using the existing EDT ownership initially (or a future queued command boundary), execute in this order:

1. Consume accepted input commands in stable `(tick, sequence)` order; revalidate placement, edits and temporary assemblies; commit permitted mutations and enqueue affected structural events. Reject out-of-world or bedrock edits deterministically.
2. Apply construction holding for assemblies still eligible at this tick; process cancellations/invalidations/expirations at a defined boundary (section 6). Holding is **not** a structural reaction.
3. Advance existing cellular materials once with the documented scan direction, seeded random stream and `moved[]`; record structural-relevant changes without altering the M1–M3 order until an approved change introduces coupling.
4. Build/update contacts in deterministic body/feature order, integrate rigid bodies for one fixed step and solve constraints using a fixed iteration count. Record contact impulses/reactions; resolve collision/occupancy by a specified conservative policy before integration is enabled. No deferred, duplicate or silently deleted mass.
5. Merge ordered edits, contact/load changes, bond/joint damage and invalidations into the structural queue. Process up to a fixed **logical work-unit** budget (e.g. bounded bond/element evaluations, value TBD); ordered keys include tick, event kind and canonical cell/body/bond IDs. Do not use elapsed milliseconds or hash-map order. Coalesce duplicate events deterministically without losing the newest revision.
6. Apply validated completed reference-solver results as an atomic revision: update persistent bond/attachment/joint damage; enqueue break events; extract newly detached components and create bodies via the transaction in section 5. Resulting geometry/contact changes enqueue work for later ticks in a stable order. Render committed state only.

**Budget and safety proposal:** a multi-tick solve reads a versioned immutable logical input; edits invalidate or restart the affected pending result, never commit results computed for an obsolete revision. Pending/unknown support must **not** be advertised as proven support for other objects or used to heal damage. Conversely do not irreversibly fracture solely because a budget is exhausted; defer that region's structural commit until analysis completes. Record backlog and work units for diagnostics, use deterministic fair ordering so a continuously disturbed region cannot silently starve unrelated work, and test backlog/restart/replay. The exact local freezing/deferred-body-coupling policy, bounded memory and fairness schedule need a runnable prototype; this proposal does not assert complete physical consistency during deferred analysis. Pause executes no logical ticks, work units, damage or construction countdown; a single-step executes one complete logical tick and one budget. Rendering frames, camera movement and elapsed wall-clock time never grant extra work.

## 5. Structural and rigid-body invariants

### Bedrock and bonds

- `BEDROCK` may occupy only `y = height - 1` and cannot be erased, replaced, damaged, fragmented or moved by any edit, brush, load, reset or physics operation. **Proposed stricter coverage assumption:** every cell of the bottom row is bedrock in an admitted structural world; whether continuous coverage is required remains open. World edges otherwise remain bounded. No material ID changes in M4.0; a future ID must be unique and explicit.
- Exactly one canonical bond represents each eligible orthogonal adjacency, including across x/y 63/64; diagonal cells have no direct bond. A broken bond carries no load. Removal, changes of endpoints or extraction invalidate adjacent bonds and wake the relevant structural region, including across chunks. An intact bond's damage is in a documented range, never decreases except through an explicit future repair operation, and is not reset by adding support or recomputing a region.
- A support path must terminate at independent bedrock or another independently anchored valid support and have sufficient finite force/torque capacity. Detect cyclic dependencies explicitly: a beam anchored solely in the same otherwise unsupported rock cannot support that rock; cycles do not gain an anchor by repetition. Topological reachability filters impossible supports but is not proof that a real load can be carried. Force and torque exchange must be balanced consistently between rock, beams and anchors under the chosen approximation; do not count the same reaction twice.
- Moderate and extreme thresholds, damage increments and integrator tolerances are test parameters, not calibrated physical values. Each committed crack/failure is deterministic for fixed state, parameters and command sequence. Structural work continues off-screen and independently of render visibility, chunk allocation and particle activity masks.

### Fragment extraction and impact

- A detached component is an orthogonally connected set of stone cells under **remaining intact bonds**, not merely touching coordinates. Its shape can be concave or contain holes; identify its cells in canonical order across chunk boundaries. Connectivity is used to extract an already-failed component, not to decide its strength.
- Conversion is transactional at a tick boundary: validate all source cells and bond revisions; derive mass and inertia from cell count, 0.1 m geometry and the chosen density; create exactly one initial rigid fragment for the component; remove exactly those cells from static world and transfer their mass, shape and relevant damage/attachments once. On validation failure, commit neither side and requeue. Never leave static duplicates, lose cells, include bedrock, or expose an intermediate state to rendering/saving. Record any intentionally broken bonds as failure state, not a second mass owner.
- Fragment rotation, collision and splitting on impact must conserve total rock mass (allowing only documented numerical tolerance), assign stable child IDs deterministically, and preserve momentum within the integrator's declared tolerance or explicitly account for external impulses. Contact with sand/water/solid cells, tunnelling, resting, and atomic re-voxelization versus permanently dynamic bodies are unresolved; do not silently overwrite or delete occupied cells. The future implementation must define collision/eviction and fallback policy with conservation tests **before** enabling such interaction.

## 6. Beams, attachments and temporary construction

**Confirmed beam geometry and commands:** beams are separate rigid bodies with 2×2-cell cross-section, arbitrary orientation, Q/E rotations of 5° and Shift+Q/E rotations of 1°. A fabricated solid beam cannot exceed its source log; the log/resource ownership and actual permissible length range remain open. Unsupported beams fall as rigid bodies. A replaceable attachment policy may automatically attach a beam to stable rock at a valid contact; contact alone must not imply infinite anchorage. Every attachment has finite force and torque limits and persistent damage. Beam–beam contact alone never makes a joint: an explicit player-installed rigid `BEAM_JOINT` is required, with finite force and torque limits and accumulating damage. End–end, end–middle and middle–middle contacts are valid subject to geometry and policy.

**Placement invariants:** snap only translation within 0.5 cell to an eligible target; never rotate automatically. Ctrl temporarily disables snapping. Local intersections are permitted only at an intended or existing joint; long collinear/near-collinear overlapping segments are invalid, even if an endpoint touches a joint. Exact overlap tolerance, eligible targets and collision geometry must be specified before UI implementation. A deterministic tie break for equally close candidates is required; invalid previews must not mutate the world, consume a log, create a joint or start a timer.

**Temporary assembly lifecycle (proposed):** construction hold lasts exactly 300 advancing simulation ticks (5 simulation seconds at 60 TPS), starting at the tick when a valid placement is committed. It may constrain only the unfinished assembly's own motion; it provides **zero load-bearing support to rock** and is not counted as an independent anchor. On each tick revalidate body existence, geometry, resource reservation and referenced contact/joint IDs. On expiry, remove the hold once, then let unsupported bodies enter ordinary fixed-step dynamics; supported bodies remain subject to finite attachment/joint capacity. Pause does not advance the timer; single-step advances it by exactly one. At the expiry boundary commands and expiration are ordered by the tick sequence in section 4; a valid completion committed before expiry ends the temporary state, otherwise expiry wins.

Cancellation is an explicit ordered command: atomically remove temporary holds and provisional connections, then return unconsumed reserved resources or place already committed beams into ordinary physics according to an explicit fabrication/ownership transaction. It must not duplicate or delete mass; whether material is refundable after fabrication is a gameplay decision. If an anchor/joint/body is removed, geometry becomes invalid or the assembly is otherwise invalidated, remove affected holds/provisional constraints deterministically at the next command boundary and transition remaining unsupported bodies to ordinary physics; show invalid state, not a permanent free support. Exact UI feedback and resource policy remain unresolved, but cancellation, pause, expiry and invalidation must be headless-testable before controls are added.

## 7. Persistence and migration (future proposal)

MSIM v1 stays readable exactly as documented in `m3-save-format.md`; v1 has no structural state and its reader rejects unknown IDs/versions. Design a **new version**, not an in-place reinterpretation of v1 bytes: retain a bounded header and CRC, add explicitly sized/versioned sections for static cells/bedrock, canonical bond and attachment/joint damage, beam and fragment IDs/shapes/poses/velocities/mass, beam provenance, and active assembly state including remaining hold ticks. Reject unsupported versions, duplicate IDs/bonds, dangling endpoints, invalid geometry, inconsistent mass and checksums before replacing the live world. Limits and actual wire layout are to be specified in M4.8. Rebuild derived indexes and verify conservatively on load; do not serialize renderer cache as authority.

**v1 migration conflict:** current v1 permits arbitrary material on the bottom row and `WOOD` cells, whereas M4 requires indestructible bedrock and separate beams. A reader must preserve v1 cell values; it must never silently replace a bottom row, reinterpret `WOOD` as beams, drop mass or claim that an incompatible file is already a valid structural world. Proposed safe policy: load as a legacy M1–M3 world with M4 structural/construction disabled, then offer a separate, explicit, transactional migration. Under the proposed continuous-bedrock assumption, a first prototype may permit conversion only when the entire bottom row is AIR; otherwise reject conversion with a clear explanation and leave the original unchanged until the user resolves it. Replacing bottom AIR by foundational bedrock is a deliberate migration operation, not preservation of an existing material. Existing `WOOD` cells remain legacy static cells unless an explicit lossless policy is later specified. Future exact simulation continuation (random state, logical tick, pending event order and partially processed solver state) is **not** provided by v1 and remains a v2 scope decision; a v2 that omits any of these must explicitly describe restart/rebuild semantics rather than promise exact continuation. Save only a consistent committed boundary; preserve active damage and temporary countdown, or explicitly reject saves during unsupported intermediate states.

## 8. Reference test scenes (proposed fixtures)

Coordinates are cells with y down; use bounded worlds, stone and a bottom bedrock row; isolate each scene, keep geometry and test-only material/load parameters fixed before implementation, and record seeds, tick budget, expected event sequence and failure tolerances. All forces, strengths and failure times below are **relative or test-only**, not final balance values. Every scene runs headlessly, with renderer visibility and storage chunk alignment varied where applicable.

| Scene | Fixture and required oracle |
|---|---|
| Narrow / wide tunnels | Same roof material, depth and thickness; compare e.g. 4-cell and 16-cell spans under the same test load. Choose a test-only load/capacity so the wide roof fails earlier while narrow remains stable for the declared horizon; preserve mass and external support reactions. |
| Thin / thick roofs | Same tunnel span and loading, one vs four stone layers. In a calibrated test regime thicker must tolerate a larger applied load or survive longer; do not assume a universal monotonic law for arbitrary geometry. |
| Stone cantilever | One end anchored to bedrock-supported stone; vary free span or tip load. Disconnected and connected-but-overloaded cases must both be distinguished; fail with intact connectivity when stress exceeds finite capacity. |
| Progressive cracking | Fixed moderate overload over several ticks produces monotone bond damage and a visible crack; reduced load or added support does not heal it. A separate extreme load triggers immediate failure at the specified tick. |
| Diagonal beam support | Rock roof contacts an oblique 2×2 beam anchored independently to stable rock; compare same roof without a beam and with insufficient anchor capacity. Finite contact reactions and force/torque limits must determine the outcome. |
| Joint failure | Two beams connected by an explicitly installed joint under known load; impose force/torque over capacity, observe accumulated damage, joint break and subsequent roof/beam response. Mere crossing/contact without installation never creates a joint. |
| Detached fragment | An isolated, irregular stone island including a hole crosses a chunk edge; bond failure extracts one body with exact cell mass, no duplicated cells, deterministic ID and repeatable rotation/impact split. Collision with fluid/occupied cells must obey the explicitly selected conservation policy. |
| Chunk boundary | Translate the same bonded fixture from within a chunk to x/y=63/64, including a four-chunk intersection; translated event sequence and results match after translation, including off-screen execution. |
| Cyclic false support | A rock island is touched by a beam attached only to that same island, optionally through a loop of joints. With no independent anchored path, neither beam nor island qualifies as grounded; removing a genuine independent anchor eventually causes failure. |
| Deterministic replay | Replay identical initial world, seed, stable IDs, fixed work budget, placement/removal commands, pause/single-step schedule and save boundary. Compare canonical state, pending work, damage, body IDs and events every tick across repeated runs; compare reference and adaptive results with predeclared tolerances, not an assumed bitwise match. |

Construction fixtures additionally cover snap distance just below/at/above 0.5 cell, Ctrl disable, 5°/1° rotations without auto-rotation, all three joint locations, forbidden long overlap, source-log length, cancellation, 299/300-tick expiry, pause, single-step, invalidation and no rock support during holding. Introduce numerical test parameters before implementing each oracle, and document fixture seeds and tolerances with the test; do not tune expected results after seeing output without review.

## 9. Roadmap: M4.1–M4.10 (proposed)

Every slice must preserve existing tests unless it explicitly introduces a reviewed behavior change; use seedable headless tests, no camera-based shortcuts and separate user-approved implementation scope. Later slices depend on earlier accepted contracts, not merely diagrams.

| Slice | Scope and dependency | Tests and acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| M4.1 | Bedrock boundary, mutation commands/events and safe legacy v1 migration policy; no stress solver. Depends on current `World`/v1. | Boundary edits, brush/clear/load bypasses, IDs, v1 bottom-row conflicts and transaction rollback; no silent material loss; old baseline tests remain available under legacy rules. |
| M4.2 | Canonical orthogonal bonds, revisions, deterministic queue and cell-resolution reference scaffold. Depends on M4.1. | Unique bonds across chunks, bond invalidation, event ordering and fixed work budget/fairness; geometry/components alone never assert mechanical stability. |
| M4.3 | Calibrated test-only load transfer, finite strength, progressive damage and cracks in reference solver. Depends on M4.2. | Narrow/wide, thin/thick, cantilever, monotone damage, no auto-heal and immediate extreme failure; declared tolerance/work convergence and deterministic replay. |
| M4.4 | Transactional detached rock extraction and headless rigid-fragment prototype. Depends on M4.3. | Exact mass/shape and cross-chunk extraction, rollback on stale revision, no bedrock/duplicates, fixed-step pose/rotation and collision conservation under documented scope. |
| M4.5 | Beam fabrication/placement geometry, resource-length constraint and temporary holding. Depends on M4.1 and M4.4 body foundation. | Q/E intent, 5°/1°, 0.5-cell translation-only snapping/Ctrl, overlap rules, 300 ticks, pause/cancel/invalidation, no load support during hold; no auto-joints. |
| M4.6 | Replaceable finite rock-attachment policy and explicit beam joints. Depends on M4.5. | All three joint locations, no joint on contact, force/torque limit and damage, deterministic tie breaks, finite anchor failures and attachment invalidation. |
| M4.7 | Coupled rock/beam/joint load reactions and independent support paths. Depends on M4.3, M4.4 and M4.6. | Diagonal support, insufficient attachment, joint-triggered collapse, equal/opposite accounted reactions, cycle rejection and off-screen propagation; mass conserved. |
| M4.8 | Versioned persistence for committed M4 state and explicit v1 import/migration. Depends on M4.1 and M4.4–M4.7. | v1 compatible import, reject incompatible migration without loss, v2 round-trip of bodies/damage/joints/hold, malformed files and atomic replacement; specify restart vs exact continuation. |
| M4.9 | Adaptive structural elements compared with M4.3 reference, without replacing it. Depends on M4.3/M4.7. | Refinement near cracks/contacts, cross-chunk fixtures and work accounting; predeclared error bounds for reactions, topology and failure timing, plus measured performance; fallback/refine on excess error. |
| M4.10 | Integrated scenario validation, construction UI and diagnostics, limited to approved M4 behavior. Depends on M4.5–M4.9. | All reference scenes, deterministic replay, pause/step and persistence, GUI interactions where available, headless test/build and benchmark; record limitations and calibrated test parameters. |

This roadmap does not require a player/inventory, mining game or external physics engine in the early slices; use test commands and fixtures until explicitly approved. `BEAM_JOINT`, construction holds, damage and `BEDROCK` are future concepts, not current material enum values or persisted IDs.

## 10. Risk register and unresolved parameters

| Risk | Mitigation / acceptance gate |
|---|---|
| Connectivity mistaken for stability or cyclic self-support | Independent anchored-path validation plus finite load/torque solver; cantilever and cyclic fixtures must fail appropriately. |
| Solver instability or false physical credibility | Cell-resolution reference, bounded iterations, explicit units and approximations, benchmark/tolerance reports; no FEM/realism claims without validation. |
| Adaptive elements hide cracks or change failure time | Refine near discontinuities; compare reference scenes and reject or refine above predeclared error bounds. |
| Budget starvation, stale work or partial failure | Stable work-unit budget, revision checks, fair queue, atomic commits and replay/backlog tests; no wall-clock budget. |
| Mass loss, double-counting or tunnelling during extraction/impact | Transactional conversion, deterministic IDs, mass/momentum accounting and explicit collision policy; no silent fluid/cell overwrite. |
| Contacts/joints jitter, tunnelling or platform-dependent floating point | Fixed timestep/iterations, stable ordering and defined tolerances; choose numerical representation after measured cross-platform tests. |
| UI holding masks unsupported rock or pause races | Structural reaction excludes construction holds; test 300 logical ticks, pause/step, cancellation and invalidation under EDT-safe commands. |
| Legacy saves contain bottom-row cells or WOOD | Preserve v1; explicit reversible migration, never silently overwrite; validate v2 references and CRC before replacement. |
| Large-world performance, chunk boundaries or off-screen omission | Cross-boundary/off-screen fixtures, headless work counters, reference comparisons; separate storage, activity, visibility and structural work. |

**Open for calibration/design:** density, gravity and units for forces/torques; stone bond stiffness, compression/tension/shear/bending capacities; damage accumulation and extreme overload thresholds; beam length range, bending model, log acquisition and resource refunds; attachment/contact tolerances, anchor policy, joint force/torque capacities and fatigue; snap tie breaks and precise overlap geometry; dynamics solver/substeps, collision response, water/sand interaction and fragmentation criteria; reference solver convergence, structural event budget, fairness and backlog policy; adaptive element geometry and allowed errors; v2 wire fields, numeric encoding, limits and exact-resume scope; visual crack mapping and accessibility; test-scene dimensions, loads and acceptance tolerances. Values are not implied by the 10 cm cell scale alone.
