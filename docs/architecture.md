# Architecture and limitations

`World` owns a dense array of eagerly allocated 64×64 storage chunks, each containing a flat `byte[]` of material IDs. The world remains bounded and fixed-size (the sandbox is 400×300); no sparse or infinite storage, streaming or persistence is present. For nonnegative world coordinates, chunk coordinates are `(x / 64, y / 64)` and local coordinates are `(x % 64, y % 64)`, with local index `localY * 64 + localX`. Edge chunks may have unused cells, which remain air and cannot be accessed as world coordinates. Bounds are checked before mapping; out-of-bounds access throws. Callers, simulation and renderer do not receive chunks or their mutable arrays. Painting and clearing deliberately replace cells; simulation movement preserves material counts.

The existing 32×32 activity tiles are a separate optional simulation bookkeeping mechanism, not storage chunks. FULL_SCAN remains the default; `World.set` and `clear` retain their activity wake-up behavior. Storage and simulation still operate through world-coordinate `get`/`set`, including across chunk edges. See `m3-chunk-performance.md` for measured overhead and limitations.

In experimental ACTIVE_REGIONS, initial activation covers all tiles. Each actual `World.set` marks its tile; at the start of the next tick, collection wakes that tile and its eight valid neighbors. Both source and destination writes from movement are marked, including liquid displacement; the 32-cell neighborhood conservatively covers the eight-cell water search. A clear wakes all tiles. A settled region may sleep after changes stop; a later edit or neighboring movement wakes it again. Changes during a tick affect the next tick's active mask, not the current one, and skipped particles do not consume random numbers. Consequently ACTIVE_REGIONS can differ from FULL_SCAN, which remains the reference. The simulation requires exclusive ownership of a world's activity tracking. See `m3-activity-propagation.md` for tests, diagnostics and measurements.

Camera visibility, render-dirty state, simulation activity and future structural propagation are distinct concerns: an off-screen region can still simulate, a visible region can be inactive, and a material edit may matter differently to each subsystem. This milestone does not implement dirty rendering, camera-driven simulation, structural propagation or a chunk scheduler.

`Material` has explicit stable IDs and categories; granular materials can displace liquid. `Simulation` is headless, runs only on explicit `step()` calls, and depends on neither Swing nor time. Seeded randomness, bottom-up traversal and a per-tick moved mask define the current discrete rules. Granular material can exchange places with water, but cannot displace water that has already moved in that tick. Water can move once per tick across up to eight contiguous air cells horizontally; see `water-flow.md`. FULL_SCAN is the default; ACTIVE_REGIONS remains experimental (`m2-activity.md`). No stable particle identities are stored; material behavior is documented in `materials.md`.

`SimulationControl` implements pause and single-step and times actual calls to `step()` outside the physics. `Main` owns the 60 Hz accumulator and caps catch-up at five ticks; elapsed time beyond that cap is dropped. Paused time is discarded. FPS, TPS and timings measure different things.

Swing's event dispatch thread (EDT) handles input, simulation, timer callbacks and painting. This avoids concurrent world mutation, but slow simulation or rendering delays input and frames. `Java2DRenderer` fills a reusable image buffer and scales it; it does not advance physics. The small `Renderer` interface nevertheless takes `Graphics2D`, so it is an abstraction between Java2D implementations, not a backend-neutral graphics API.

`Camera2D` is independent of Swing: its origin is the world-space coordinate at the viewport's top-left, x increases right and y increases down, and zoom is screen pixels per world cell. Screen coordinates map to origin + screen/zoom; cells are selected with floor, including for negative coordinates. Visible cell bounds are half-open and clipped to the fixed world. The camera can pan outside the world, where the renderer draws a background and the brush ignores pointer positions; the brush radius is in world cells. Wheel zoom preserves the world point under the cursor, including when zoom reaches its limits. The renderer updates visible cells in the reusable image and applies a single nearest-neighbor transform; subpixel zoom necessarily omits some cells. Camera and brush do not advance simulation.

An eventual LWJGL/OpenGL implementation will need a different rendering boundary and appropriate window/context ownership, input and thread coordination; the current interface cannot directly support it. Material rules need not depend on that choice. No such redesign, concurrency, streaming or GPU physics is introduced here.
