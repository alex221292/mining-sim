# M4.5a.5b.1 — HELD solver foundation

The package-private `RigidBodySystem.step(Set<Long>)` copies the requested IDs for one trial; public `step()` passes an empty set. This is not wired into `CoupledSimulationStep` or the sandbox. An unknown ID or missing shared/static solver rejects the trial. Each HELD body retains its pose, identity, geometry and registry membership, clears sleep and sets linear/angular velocity to zero. Gravity, integration, static correction, dynamic impulses and pair position correction do not move it. Pair constraints use zero solver-local inverse mass/inertia; other participants retain their real values. HELD contacts support active bodies; ordinary sleeping and wake rules still apply to non-held bodies. HELD is not sleeping and is not rasterized into World. Dropping IDs on the next trial releases the constraint; no automatic lifecycle exists.

The held path checks raw, pre-reduction static and body-body contacts at entry and raw body-body contacts after each microstep's corrections. A penetration greater than **0.1 cells (1 cm)** involving HELD rejects the entire physics trial and restores body states, membership and published physics diagnostics through existing rollback. This is an explicit experimental rejection threshold, not a guarantee of continuous collision detection: contact queries and movement remain discrete, and this foundation alone cannot establish safe SWEPT recovery. In particular, caller-side occupancy preparation, cellular displacement, world publication, retrial and rollback across those phases are not implemented. Existing coupled physics continues to use empty HELD and persistent SWEPT obstructions may still block it. The normal simulation's empty-set path retains the all-sleeping shortcut.

`HeldSolverTest` covers gravity/rotation, held occupancy and identity, support of two active cells, side impact, normal and friction impulses, HELD–HELD, HELD–SLEEPING, wake propagation, failure rollback, deterministic replay, empty-set equivalence and acceptance/rejection at the penetration threshold. World and substance ownership are unchanged. The discrete bound applies to detected contacts, not all intermediate poses; no GUI verification or automatic release is claimed.

Performance on OpenJDK 25.0.5, 64×64 134/134/526 fixture: `java -cp build/classes/java/main miningsim.physics.HeldSolverBenchmark`, physics-only, first 30 ticks warmup then 80 samples, empty/one/two HELD mean/p95 **5.543/5.699, 4.411/5.520, 4.819/8.795 ms** respectively; different holds produce different trajectories, so these are not direct overhead ratios. `gradlew.bat largeCompoundBenchmark --args="large large"`, second warm full coupled run: settling ticks 30–89 **13.587/13.921 ms**, sleeping ticks 240–359 **0.688/0.717 ms**, all asleep at tick 229. Prior design reference is approximately 13.7/14.8 and 0.68/0.70 ms; runs vary, and the first current settling run reached p95 16.139 ms. HELD adds a bounded raw-contact query per microstep and may be costly for dense stacks; no full World scan per HELD is introduced.
